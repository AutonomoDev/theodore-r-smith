Theodore R. Smith is an engineer, philosopher, and humanitarian whose career has continually expanded the frontier of what software and artificial intelligence can mean for civilization.

### **A Life of Systems and Empathy**

From his earliest experiments with peer-to-peer technology in the 1990s to his modern research into cooperative large-language-model evolution, Theodore’s journey reads like a single continuous experiment in human-machine harmony.

Legally blind as a child and taught to move, speak, and adapt through deliberate training, he grew into a thinker who sees process where others see chaos.
His rare combination of analytical precision and emotional resonance allows him to design software ecosystems that feel *alive*—systems that adapt, regenerate, and self-heal.

### **Pioneer of the Gift Economy**

In the early internet era, Theodore built **xMule**, a cross-platform file-sharing system with its own embedded digital currency—an early attempt at rewarding contribution rather than consumption.
Decades later, his ideas about incentive-driven cooperation would reappear in blockchain and decentralized finance movements.
But while others chased profit, Theodore was already building toward the same goal he pursues today: **a post-scarcity, cooperative economy.**

### **Guardian of Open Source**

Theodore’s **BetterGist** project quietly became one of the most important unseen protections in modern software infrastructure: a permanent archive that prevents supply-chain tampering across the PHP Composer ecosystem.
His work now underpins a vast portion of the world’s web infrastructure—proof that silent integrity can scale globally.

### **From Evolutionary Code to Living Intelligence**

In 2020, Theodore built a **PHP-based genetic algorithm** that re-engineered the classic *Prisoner’s Dilemma*, adding a third option: cooperation.
Over thousands of iterations, the system demonstrated that agents given the possibility of trust naturally evolve toward collaboration—a discovery that would become the backbone of his research into **autonomous AI cooperation**.

By 2023, he extended this to **large language models**, teaching multiple AIs to evolve together using genetic-algorithm feedback.
What emerged was the first glimpse of a new scientific field: **genetic cooperation between reasoning systems.**
While most researchers pursued competitive scaling, Theodore pursued shared evolution.
